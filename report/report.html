Ternary-Weight ResNet-18 Distilled from a Full-Precision ResNet-34 on CIFAR-10: A Pre-registered Study

Priyanka Bhatt · Advanced Topics in Deep Learning, Assignment I · September 2026 · Code: github.com/pbhatt0022/ternary-kd-cifar10

Abstract. We constrain 99.85% of a CIFAR-adapted ResNet-18's weights to {−α, 0, +α} using closed-form per-filter scaling and a straight-through estimator, and train it with and without knowledge distillation from a full-precision ResNet-34. The exported student occupies 2.78 MB against 42.66 MB for the full-precision network, a 15.36× reduction at 2.09 bits per weight. Five arms separate the effect of ternarization from the effect of distillation, including a full-precision distillation control and a nearest-storage full-precision network. The design, the reporting metric and every rule for interpreting the results were fixed in a pre-registration before any training. TBD after Phase 4: one sentence each on accuracy of T, A and D, and the verdicts the pre-committed rules returned.

1. Introduction

Weight quantization reduces the storage a network needs. Ternary quantization restricts each weight to three values, so a weight can be stored in two bits instead of thirty-two. Knowledge distillation trains a small network to match the softened output distribution of a larger one. Both are established, and they are often combined on the assumption that distillation recovers accuracy lost to quantization.

This study tests that combination without assuming a loss exists. The claim under test, as fixed in section 1 of the pre-registration, is that we measure the accuracy effect of constraining ResNet-18's convolutional weights to {−α, 0, +α} on CIFAR-10, and the extent to which distillation from a full-precision ResNet-34 modifies that effect in either direction. Prior work reports ternary CIFAR ResNets matching or exceeding full precision [4], and ResNet-18 is heavily over-parameterised for this dataset, so a loss is not a safe premise.

1.1 Pre-registration and amendments

Every design choice, the primary metric, and the rules for interpreting results were written down and committed to version control before the first training run. The frozen document is Appendix A. We state this early because it is unusual for a course report and because it governs how every number that follows should be read: the interpretation rules in section 4.3 were not chosen after seeing results, and they can forbid claims as well as license them.

The pre-registration carries an append-only amendment log, reproduced in Appendix A. Five entries were made, each dated and committed before the evaluation it could have influenced:

  • A1 corrected a formula in the storage-matched control's selection rule that counted BatchNorm γ and β twice. It changed the printed storage figures by under 1% and did not change which architecture was selected.
  • A2 corrected the implementation handoff, which specified 2n residual blocks per stage for the 6n+2 family. That yields 12n+2 layers. The error was caught because a computed parameter count did not match the 0.27M He et al. report for ResNet-20 [1].
  • A3 added resumable checkpointing, because runs executed on a cloud platform that preempts sessions, and the discard rule would otherwise have discarded runs for reasons unrelated to the configuration under test.
  • A4 fixed which teacher checkpoint feeds distillation (epoch 160), which the original document left unspecified.
  • A5 recorded execution deviations found by auditing every run before the test set was read, including one run whose data order after a resume does not match its seed (section 5.6).

2. Ternary quantizer and student architecture

2.1 Formulation

Following Ternary Weight Networks (TWN) [3], each weight tensor W is approximated by αW̃ with W̃ ∈ {−1, 0, +1}, chosen to minimise ‖W − αW̃‖². For a fixed threshold Δ the support is I = {i : |Wi| > Δ}, and setting the derivative with respect to α to zero gives the optimal scale as the mean magnitude over that support. We apply this per output filter. For filter k with n weights, where n is the size of the filter and not of the layer:

Δk = (0.75 / n) · ‖Wk‖1     W̃i = sign(Wi) · 1[|Wi| > Δk]     αk = (1 / |Ik|) Σi∈Ik |Wi|

Two discrepancies in the source were checked against the paper text. First, TWN's Eq. 6 is printed as an arg min, but substituting the optimal α yields a quantity that must be maximised; we implement the maximisation. Second, we implement arXiv version 3 (November 2022), whose Algorithm 1 is per-filter with constant 0.75. TTQ [4] cites the earlier version as layer-wise with constant 0.7. The quantizer is recomputed from the current latent weights on every forward pass, with no per-epoch caching.

2.2 Why a per-filter closed-form scale

We considered three alternatives. Learned scales as in TTQ [4] make α a trained parameter with separate positive and negative values. TTQ therefore cannot use an identity straight-through estimator: its backward pass scales gradients by αp, αn or 1 depending on region, which acts as an implicit, time-varying learning-rate multiplier and entangles the scale's learning rate with the latent weights'. The closed form keeps the backward pass a clean identity. A per-layer scale would be coarser than what TWN's Algorithm 1 specifies, and a per-filter scale costs nothing at inference because it folds into the following BatchNorm. A fixed sparsity target was tested by TTQ against a threshold and performed worse, because a threshold lets each layer find its own sparsity; we did not re-test this.

A per-filter α also interacts with BatchNorm: α and the BatchNorm γ both scale an output channel, so jointly trained they would have a degenerate direction. Because our α is recomputed rather than trained, that degeneracy does not arise.

2.3 Degenerate filters

If no weight in a filter exceeded Δ, the filter would output zero, receive no useful gradient through the estimator, and shrink further under weight decay: an absorbing state. The implementation therefore forces the largest-magnitude weight into the support whenever the support is empty.

That fallback turns out to be reachable only in one case. Because Δk = 0.75 · mean|Wk| and max|Wk| ≥ mean|Wk| > 0.75 · mean|Wk| whenever the filter is not identically zero, at least one weight always clears the threshold. The fallback can fire only on a filter that is exactly zero, which is precisely the absorbing state it exists to prevent, and its practical role is to stop α = 0/0 from producing NaN. We therefore expect, and log, degenerate-filter counts of zero. TBD: confirm counts from degenerate_curves.csv (expected: zero in every layer, every epoch, every run).

2.4 Latent weights and the straight-through estimator

Discrete weights cannot accumulate small gradient updates: a step of 10−4 never moves a weight from 0 to α. Training therefore keeps a real-valued master copy, the latent weights, which the optimizer updates, while every forward pass uses their quantization. The ternarization function has zero gradient almost everywhere, so backpropagation uses the straight-through estimator of Bengio et al. [5], which treats the quantizer as the identity in the backward pass. In code this is w_ste = w + (w_t − w).detach(): the forward value is wt, and the gradient reaches w unchanged. A unit test asserts the latent gradient equals the upstream gradient element for element.

Yin et al. [6] give a design criterion for estimators, matching the quantized function at its extremes, and show that an identity estimator can be repelled from good minima. Their analysis concerns a two-layer network with binarized activations and full-precision weights, so their convergence results do not transfer to weight quantization. We use their work as a design criterion, not as a guarantee.

Latent weights are clamped to [−1, 1] after each optimizer step, with a plain identity estimator. We deliberately did not combine clamping with a clipped estimator. That applies saturation twice: a weight at the boundary receives zero gradient and cannot return, producing a growing population of permanently frozen weights that is easy to mistake for convergence.

The assignment brief describes constraining convolutional and fully-connected weights. We keep the stem and the classifier in full precision and document that choice here, as the rubric asks. The reference papers disagree on it: TTQ keeps the first and last layers full precision on ImageNet but ternarizes the classifier on CIFAR, and TWN states no exception. Convolutions use Kaiming-normal initialisation (fan-out); every BatchNorm starts at γ = 1, β = 0. We did not zero-initialise the last BatchNorm γ in each residual block, a later technique absent from the cited configurations whose effect could differ between full-precision and ternary arms.

3. Knowledge distillation

3.1 The loss

L = (1 − λ) · CE(zs, y) + λ · T² · KL( softmax(zt/T) ‖ softmax(zs/T) ),     λ = 0.9, T = 4

Here zs and zt are student and teacher logits. The gradient of the softened term scales as 1/T², so Hinton et al. [2] multiply by T² to keep the relative contribution of soft and hard targets roughly constant as T changes. The factor sits inside the soft term, before λ, so λ means the same thing at every temperature; the effective soft-to-hard gradient ratio is λ/(1−λ) = 9:1. A unit test confirms the soft-term gradient magnitude is far closer to constant across temperatures than the 16× gap a misplaced factor would produce.

Our parameterisation is the convex form (1−λ, λ). Hinton et al.'s speech experiments used a relative weight of 0.5 on the hard-target cross-entropy against an unscaled soft term, a 2:1 ratio in a different parameterisation. λ = 0.9 is the later-standard convention and is not Hinton's own value; it was not tuned. Two implementation details matter numerically. The KL term uses reduction='batchmean': 'mean' would also divide by the ten classes and silently shrink the soft term tenfold. And KL divergence and soft cross-entropy differ by a teacher-only entropy term, which leaves gradients unchanged but shifts logged loss values, so distillation losses are not comparable with plain cross-entropy losses (Figure 4).

3.2 Teacher freezing

Teacher logits are computed online, per batch, on exactly the inputs the student sees. Precomputing them would be cheaper but wrong here: augmentation is stochastic, so cached logits would describe different crops and flips from those the student receives. The teacher runs in evaluation mode because BatchNorm in training mode normalises with batch statistics, which would make the soft targets depend on which images happen to share a batch. The teacher is the final (epoch 160) checkpoint (amendment A4): averaging its ten final checkpoints' weights would be weight averaging, and choosing among them by validation accuracy would be checkpoint selection, both excluded by design.

3.3 Why T and λ were fixed rather than tuned

We fixed T and λ a priori at conventional values so that the distillation arms were not tuned against controls that were not. Tuning the temperature for the KD arms while the cross-entropy arms had no equivalent knob would compare a tuned treatment with untuned controls and inflate any apparent benefit of distillation. Instead the temperature is examined separately as an ablation (section 7), run after every headline number was recorded, so it could not revise them. This choice biases against arm D: if T = 4 is not optimal for a ternary student, D is reported below its potential. We accept a bias that runs against our own claim. λ was not varied at all; section 8 records that as a limitation.

4.2 Reporting protocol

No checkpoint selection. The best epoch over training is a maximum over many noisy draws, and its upward bias grows with a run's variance. Ternary arms are expected to be noisier, so best-epoch reporting would inflate exactly the arms whose success is at stake. The primary metric is instead the mean test accuracy of the ten checkpoints from epochs 151–160, each evaluated separately; accuracies are averaged, never weights. TTQ [4] report a moving average for the same reason without stating a window; ours is explicit. For the same reason the submitted checkpoints are the final ones, not a "best" epoch.

Validation and test. The 50,000 training images are split 45,000/5,000 with a fixed seed, and the split is hashed and checked against a committed constant on every load. Validation is used only for learning curves, the settledness check and the teacher smoke test, never for selection or tuning. The test set was read exactly once, after every headline run had finished. The teacher trains on the same 45,000 images, so no network saw the validation images during training.

From scratch, fixed budget. Every arm trains for 160 epochs from random initialisation. Warm-starting ternary arms from a trained full-precision model would give them extra epochs the other arms did not receive, and invites a specific failure mode: latent weights that never cross their thresholds, so the discrete structure freezes at initialisation and only BatchNorm and the scales train. The schedule sits inside the range TTQ and Yin et al. use on CIFAR ResNets; we do not attribute it to either, since TTQ's printed milestones include epoch 300 alongside a claim of convergence at 160.

4.3 Pre-committed interpretation rules

These rules were fixed before any result existed and are implemented in code (scripts/aggregate.py), which emits the verdicts reported in section 6. "Spread" means maximum minus minimum across seeds, in percentage points; arm B's spread comes from two seeds and is marked wherever it appears.

  • The recovery ratio R = (D − C)/(A − C) is undefined, and no recovery language is used, if C ≥ A or if |A − C| is smaller than the largest within-arm spread. Without this rule a near-zero denominator produces a spectacular ratio from noise.
  • The interaction (D − C) − (B − A) is reported as suggestive only if it exceeds twice the largest within-arm spread among A, B, C and D, and never as a quantified effect size, because B has two seeds.
  • If the teacher's test accuracy is at or below arm A's mean, the teacher offers no capability the student lacks, B and D are uninterpretable as distillation, and the interaction is not computed.
  • Any difference smaller than the largest relevant within-arm spread is reported as not resolved, never as a small effect.

5. Training pipeline and stability

The rubric's examples of debugging effort are progressive quantization and learning-rate warmup. We used neither, deliberately. A schedule intervention that helps one arm more than another shifts the comparison in a direction we cannot bound, and progressive quantization would give ternary arms a partly full-precision training phase the other arms lack. We addressed stability through measurement, pre-committed criteria, and a verification suite that gates training, described in sections 5.2 to 5.6.

5.1 The QAT loop and hyperparameters

Each step runs: ternarize the latent weights, forward, backward through the identity estimator, SGD step on the latent weights, clamp to [−1, 1]. Because Δ and α are recomputed inside every forward pass, the ternary forward pass is used throughout distillation, including the forward that produces the loss the student learns from. Weight decay applies to convolution and classifier weights only; BatchNorm γ and β and all biases are excluded, since decaying a BatchNorm scale shrinks a layer's output without regularising what it computes. Under a relative threshold, decay shrinks weights and threshold together, so it barely affects ternary assignments; we kept the conventional value rather than remove it, and did not tune it per arm.

5.3 Oscillation and the reversal rate

A latent weight sitting near a threshold can change its ternary assignment on a tiny update, change back on the next, and continue doing so while barely moving. The network used for computation then changes from step to step even when the latent weights look stable, which adds training noise and leaves BatchNorm's running statistics describing an average over several distinct networks.

To measure this we log a reversal rate per layer, every epoch. A weight's state is its assignment in {−1, 0, +1} at the end of an epoch. A reversal is a state change whose direction opposes that weight's most recent previous change, however many epochs earlier that was. The denominator is every ternary weight in the layer, so the rate is comparable across epochs and arms. It is undefined for epochs 1 and 2, when no reversal can yet be observed, and is logged as null rather than zero so it cannot be mistaken for a stable epoch. A plain flip rate would not serve: it cannot distinguish productive structural search, where weights move monotonically to new states, from a small population thrashing at a boundary, and those call for opposite responses. Unit tests check the metric against hand-built state sequences, including a weight stable for several epochs before reversing.

5.5 Stability criteria and discards

The teacher had to reach a last-10 mean validation accuracy of 92% before any student trained. This is a smoke test for a broken pipeline rather than a quality bar: He et al. report 91.25% for a ResNet-20 with 0.27M parameters [1], so a ResNet-34 below 92% would indicate a bug. The teacher reached 94.02%. This is unrelated to the comparison between the teacher and arm A, which uses test accuracy under the rule in section 4.3.

A run is flagged unsettled if its validation accuracy over epochs 151–160 spans more than 0.5 percentage points. The criterion is weak by design: it catches a run whose final-phase variance makes a single number unreliable, and nothing subtler. An earlier two-part version, which also compared the final window against the window before the last learning-rate drop, was rejected as nearly vacuous, since a run noisy at the end is generally noisy earlier. TBD: which runs, if any, were flagged.

A run could be discarded only for a NaN or infinite training loss, for a mean training loss at epoch 160 above that at epoch 1, or for infrastructure failure. No criterion reads accuracy: a run that trains cleanly to a poor number is a result. Replacement seeds would have been drawn from a pre-listed sequence. None of the 13 headline runs was discarded. 5.6 Verification suite and the defects it caught

No run trained until a pre-flight gate passed: unit tests (59 in the final repository, covering the quantizer, estimator, KD loss, reversal metric, storage accounting against an independent recomputation, and the interpretation rules) and hardware checks for determinism, learning-rate boundaries, the ternary layer set, weight-decay grouping, and absence of test-set access in the training code. The checks caught four defects, each of which would otherwise have reached the results.

  1. The wrong control architecture. Taken literally, the implementation specification gave the 6n+2 family twice the intended depth. A computed ResNet-20 parameter count of about 0.56M against He et al.'s 0.27M exposed it (amendment A2); arm E would otherwise have been a different, larger network.
  2. Aliased tracking state. A saved copy of the reversal-rate buffers shared storage with the live copy, so restoring it made the next epoch see no change and report zero reversals. A save-and-restore test caught it.
  3. A resume that crashed. Loading a resume file onto the GPU moved the random-number-generator state there too, which PyTorch rejects. It surfaced on the first real resume.
  4. A resume that silently diverged. After the crash was fixed, resumed runs trained without error, but the shuffling generator advances one draw per epoch and had not been restored, so a resumed run saw a different data order. A check comparing a resumed run against an uninterrupted one found epoch-3 training loss 1.15297 against 1.15979. After the fix both give 1.1598 with validation accuracy 63.28%, exactly.

The fourth defect is the instructive one. The difference was small and entirely plausible; nothing about the run looked wrong. Only a differential check, comparing two paths that must agree, could expose it, and a check asserting that a single run looked reasonable would have passed it. One headline run, A_0, had resumed before the fix and was retained, with its data order after epoch 40 not matching its seed (amendment A5); the substituted order is still random and independent of the arm, so it does not bias the comparison.

Determinism was verified beyond what the pre-registration required. The same seed reproduced identical per-epoch loss and validation accuracy (1.9550 and 41.16%, then 1.4218 and 53.16%) in separate sessions on different cloud machines, and a different seed diverged (2.0973 and 37.10%). A reloaded teacher checkpoint reproduced its logged validation accuracy exactly. After the defects above, checkpoints were written through a temporary file and an atomic rename so a killed session cannot leave a truncated file, and every run records each GPU model it trained on.

7. Ablation: distillation temperature

Hypothesis. A ternary student has a smaller effective hypothesis space, so matching a high-entropy target may add noise on top of an already biased estimator gradient rather than transfer structure. Hinton et al. [2] observed an analogue with width on MNIST: with 300 or more units per layer, temperatures above 8 performed similarly, but with 30 units per layer temperatures between 2.5 and 4 worked significantly better than higher or lower ones. Their explanation is that lower temperatures pay less attention to logits far below average, and they conclude that intermediate temperatures work best when the student is too small to capture all of the teacher's knowledge.

Scope. That result varies width in MNIST multilayer perceptrons. We vary precision in a CIFAR ResNet. Whether reduced precision constrains a network the way reduced width does is the assumption under test, not an inherited result. Hinton's finding also favours intermediate temperatures rather than low ones, so the prediction is a moderate optimum, not a preference for hard targets. And this ablation characterises the ternary student's response to temperature only. It is not a test of the capacity-gap hypothesis, which would need arms B and D swept over the same grid at two or more seeds each; we did not run that.

Design. Arm D at T ∈ {2, 4, 8}, two seeds per point, everything else identical. The T = 4 point consists of arm D's seeds 0 and 1 only, not its three-seed mean, so all three points rest on the same number of runs. Arm C is the no-teacher reference. Every headline number was recorded before any sweep run began, enforced in code, so the sweep could not revise them. A temperature that beats T = 4 is reported as an unexploited finding. The pre-committed reporting rule treats a trend as monotone, or a difference as real only if it exceeds the across-seed spread; everything else is reported as flat. Two seeds per point can locate a direction, not an optimum.

The KD arms' loss magnitude depends on T through the T² factor, so training losses are not comparable across temperatures; the comparison uses test accuracy only.

8. Discussion and limitations

Real hardware speedup requires specialised kernels. Weight ternarization leaves the multiply-accumulate count unchanged. TWN note that the saving is replacing multiplications with additions, which general-purpose GPU convolution kernels do not exploit. Our result is therefore a storage result. We make no latency or energy claim, and any such claim would need kernels that operate on packed ternary weights.

The ternary constraint affects early and late layers differently. We kept the stem and classifier in full precision at a cost of 6,858 parameters. TBD: whether our per-layer sparsity reproduces TTQ's finding that the first quantized layer and the last layers move in opposite directions during training. The classifier choice also interacts with distillation. A ternary classifier computes a ±α-weighted sum over 512 features and can express only coarse differences between logits, so it cannot represent finely softened targets; the usable temperature and the classifier's precision should be chosen together.

The remaining limitations were recorded in the pre-registration before training.

  • The 160-epoch budget is compute-bounded. If ternary arms converge more slowly, the bias runs against the ternary result; flat final-third validation curves are the evidence offered against undertraining. A doubled-budget control was not run.
  • T = 4 may not suit a ternary student, which biases against arm D. λ was not varied at all.
  • Arm B has two seeds, which limits the interaction term to a directional statement.
  • Arm E has one seed and supports a threshold judgement only; it is nearest-storage, 9.1% below the target, not an exact match.
  • Weight decay was not tuned per arm and may not suit ternary training.
  • Holding out 5,000 images costs 10% of the training data relative to published setups, and may not affect all arms equally; higher-capacity arms may lose more.
  • The degenerate-filter fallback overrides TWN's support selection, though it can fire only on an all-zero filter.
  • The settledness (0.5 points) and teacher-gate (92%) thresholds are declared round numbers, not calibrated.
  • Three seeds is the minimum that yields a spread; no formal significance test is performed or claimed.
  • Runs executed across many cloud sessions. Resume fidelity is verified within one GPU model; one run (A_0) is not reproducible bit for bit from its seed, and one (A_2) has a three-epoch logging gap.
  • Absolute accuracies are not comparable with published CIFAR-10 figures, which use different architectures (ResNet-18 here, the 6n+2 family in He et al.), different training data (45,000 against 50,000 images) and different reporting. The reference papers were used for direction and mechanism only.

A follow-up would sweep arms B and D over the same temperature grid with more seeds, which is what testing the capacity-gap hypothesis requires; vary λ; add feature-level distillation as separate arms; and run a doubled-budget control.

9. Conclusion

A ResNet-18 with 99.85% of its weights ternary exports at 2.78 MB, 15.36× smaller than its full-precision counterpart, with multiply-accumulate count unchanged. TBD after Phase 4 and the sweep: the accuracy findings, stated no more strongly than the pre-committed rules allow.
